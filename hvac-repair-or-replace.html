Repair or replace your air conditioner?

The rule everyone quotes is wrong in this climate. Here's a better way to decide.

Advertisement

The common rule, and why it's incomplete

Most advice says replace the system when a repair costs more than a third of a new unit, or when the equipment passes 12 to 15 years. Both parts matter, but in Central Texas the age threshold should be lower, because your system works harder than almost anywhere in the country.

A unit in Austin runs 2,500 to 3,000 hours a year. The same unit in Ohio runs under 800. At 12 years old here, your system has done the equivalent of 40 years of work in a cooler state.

The rule of thumb I'd use here: past 10 years old, with a repair over $1,500, replace. Under 10 years, repair unless the compressor or the coil has failed, which are the two parts expensive enough to tip the maths.

Repairs worth doing

  • Capacitor or contactor failure. Usually $150 to $400 installed, and a common failure in high heat. Always repair.
  • Blower motor. $400 to $900. Usually worth repairing on a system under 10 years.
  • Refrigerant leak, small and findable. $400 to $1,200 including the leak search. Repair if the unit is otherwise sound, though older R-22 systems get expensive to recharge because that refrigerant is no longer produced.
  • Condensate drain blockage. Cheap to clear, and a common cause of water damage in attics. Deal with it immediately.

Repairs that aren't worth it

  • Compressor failure on a system over 10 years old. The part plus labour approaches half the cost of new equipment, and you still have an old system at the end.
  • Evaporator coil replacement on an old R-22 system. The refrigerant cost alone can be brutal.
  • A second major repair in the same year. Two failures usually means the system is at the end of its life.

The efficiency angle

If you're replacing anyway, the efficiency gain is real but often oversold. A system going from SEER 10 to SEER 16 cuts cooling energy by roughly a third, and in Central Texas that's a meaningful monthly saving because you run the system so much. Ask for the projected saving in dollars based on your actual usage, not a percentage.

Practical steps

  • Get a written diagnosis naming the failed part and its part number.
  • Ask for both a repair price and a replacement price, in writing, at the same visit.
  • Check whether your electricity provider offers rebates for high-efficiency equipment. Several in Texas do.
  • If you replace, ask about a maintenance plan. Annual service is what keeps a new system inside its warranty.
