Hail damage and roof insurance claims in Texas

A storm rolls through, the neighbourhood fills with roofers, and everyone has an opinion. Here's the order that works.

Advertisement

Step 1: document before you call anyone

  • Note the date of the storm, and photograph anything visible: downed limbs, dents in gutters and vents, cracked skylights, granules in gutters.
  • Photograph the interior ceiling for any staining. That's evidence water got in.
  • Save any contractor flyers left on your door with the date visible.

Step 2: understand your deductible first

This is the part that surprises people. In Texas, wind and hail deductibles are usually set as a percentage of your home's insured value, not a flat amount. On a home insured for $400,000 with a 2% wind/hail deductible, you pay the first $8,000 of the roof yourself. A 1% deductible halves that.

Check your policy before filing. If your deductible is close to the cost of the roof, the claim may not be worth the impact on your premium. This is a personal financial decision, and an insurance agent can walk you through it.

Step 3: file, then collect quotes

You can gather contractor estimates at any point, but do not sign a contract before your insurer has assessed the damage. Insurers sometimes work from their own scope and price. If you've already signed, you lose the ability to negotiate that scope.

Step 4: the adjuster meeting

Be there, or have your contractor there. Walk the roof with the adjuster. Ask what was and wasn't included in the scope, in plain words. It is normal and reasonable to ask for an itemised copy.

Step 5: if the scope falls short

If the insurer's scope misses something — decking, flashing, matching shingles, code upgrades — ask your contractor to document what's needed and submit it. This is a routine part of the process, not a confrontation.

Red flags when contractors come knocking after a storm

  • Anyone offering to waive your deductible. In Texas, a contractor cannot legally pay, absorb or rebate an insurance deductible. That is a specific statutory offence, and it is the clearest signal to walk away.
  • Pressure to sign today because the price goes up tomorrow.
  • No local address, a mobile-only number, or a crew that arrived from out of state for the storm.
  • Requests for a large cash deposit up front. A deposit for materials is normal, but cash with no contract is not.
  • Unmarked trucks and no verifiable licence or insurance.

Choosing a roofer

  • Ask for their Texas licence or registration where applicable, plus proof of general liability insurance.
  • Ask how long they've operated in this area and for two local references from the past year.
  • Get the scope in writing, including decking price per sheet and whether the price includes permit and final inspection.
  • Ask what happens if it rains mid-job. A reputable roofer will have an answer.
