Sewer line inspection before you buy

On a house built before 1990, the sewer line is the single most expensive thing a standard inspection won't check. Here's what a camera survey finds and what it costs.

The short answer

A sewer scope inspection costs $250 to $600 in the Austin and San Antonio area. Against an $8,000 drain line replacement, it's the best-value test available on an older home.

ServiceTypical cost
Sewer camera inspection, residential$250 – $600
Inspection plus locate and marking$400 – $800
Second opinion on an existing recording$100 – $250

Why it matters more here

A large share of Central Texas housing stock went up between 1970 and 1995, when cast iron and early plastic drain lines were the norm. Cast iron from that era is now at or past its service life, and early plastic lines can suffer from joint failures. Meanwhile the soil in this region moves with moisture, which flexes buried pipe and opens joints. A general home inspection looks at what's visible and accessible. It does not put a camera down the sewer.

Do it before your option period ends. A sewer scope is worthless if you discover a $12,000 problem after your option period closes and you've lost the right to walk away or renegotiate. This is a timing issue as much as a technical one.

What the camera finds

  • Root intrusion at joints, which is common where mature trees stand over the line.
  • Scale and corrosion inside cast iron, narrowing the pipe until it clogs.
  • Bellies and sags, where the pipe has settled so waste pools instead of flowing.
  • Cracks, offset joints and separations from soil movement.
  • Previous patch jobs that may or may not have been done well.
  • The cleanout location, which you will want to know the day something goes wrong.

How to read the report

  • Ask for the recording, not just a summary page. Most inspectors provide it, and a plumber can review it later.
  • Ask how many feet were surveyed, and whether the camera reached the city main or the septic tank.
  • Minor scale or one small root cluster on an otherwise sound line is maintenance, not a defect.
  • A belly, a major crack, or roots through a joint is a real negotiating point.
  • A line the camera couldn't get through tells you something by itself: a blockage that stops a camera will stop waste.

What to do with a bad report

  • Get a repair quote before your option period ends. A written number turns an opinion into a negotiating position.
  • Ask for a price reduction or a repair credit rather than walking away as a first move, unless the seller won't engage.
  • Compare like for like. A quote for a spot repair and a quote for a full trenchless replacement are different conversations.
  • Know that some sellers insure this. Home warranty and sewer line coverage products exist, though they rarely cover pre-existing conditions you already know about.
