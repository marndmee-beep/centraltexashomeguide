Whole-home re-pipe cost in Central Texas

When the plumbing in a 1980s house starts failing line by line, replacing all of it is often cheaper than chasing leaks. Here's the maths.

The short answer

A full re-pipe on a typical single-family home in this region runs $4,000 to $15,000, with most homes landing between $6,000 and $10,000. The range is wide because it depends on the material, how the lines are routed, and whether the walls need opening and patching.

Copper or PEX?

Copper lasted 30 to 50 years in most of the country, which is why it was the standard. In Central Texas it tends to fail at the shorter end, because the water is hard and carries dissolved minerals that slowly attack the inside of the pipe from the joint outward.

PEX costs roughly half as much installed, can be routed through the attic without breaking out floors, and handles this region's water chemistry better. The trade-offs people mention — taste, UV exposure, and perception at resale — are real but minor for indoor installation. For most homes here, PEX through the attic is the sensible choice, and it's worth asking any plumber who insists on copper why, and what the extra money buys you.

Ask about attic heat. Attic temperatures in August can exceed 140°F. PEX is rated well above that, but the installer should still keep lines off the decking and away from direct sun near vents. Ask how they handle it.

Do you actually need one?

  • Two or more leaks in a year on the same system is the strongest signal. The remaining pipe is the same age as the pipe that just failed.
  • A home built before 1990 still on original copper, with a rising water bill and recurring pinhole leaks, is a candidate.
  • Grey or blue-green staining at fixtures, or metallic-tasting water, points to internal corrosion.
  • Low pressure at every tap can mean a failing galvanised or heavily scaled system, though it can also be a single blocked line, which is much cheaper to fix.

One leak on a 2010 house is not a re-pipe. If you're being quoted one on that basis, get a second opinion. See our slab leak guide for what a single repair should cost.

What the job involves

  • Lines are run through the attic to each fixture, with drops down interior walls.
  • Small access holes are cut in drywall at each fixture and patched afterwards.
  • The old lines are typically abandoned in place rather than removed, which is normal and not a problem.
  • The water heater connection, hose bibs and irrigation are part of the scope — confirm each one in writing.
  • Expect two to five days of work, and a day or two of patching and painting afterwards that most plumbers do not include.

Questions to ask

  • Is the price fixed, or hourly with an estimate?
  • Does it include the water heater, hose bibs and any exterior lines?
  • Who patches and paints the drywall, and is that in the price?
  • What warranty comes with the work, and does it transfer if I sell the house?
  • Will you pull the permit? Plumbing permits are required in most Central Texas jurisdictions.
