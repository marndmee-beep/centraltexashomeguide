Tree removal and storm damage

Live oaks, pecans and elms define this region, and they also drop limbs on roofs. What removal costs, and the rules that catch homeowners out.

The short answer

Removing a medium tree of 40 to 60 feet in the Austin and San Antonio area costs $800 to $2,500. A large tree over 80 feet, or one close to a house or power line, runs $2,500 to $7,000. Stump grinding adds $150 to $600 on top.

What drives the price

  • Proximity to structures and lines. Working over a roof or a power line needs rigging, sometimes a crane, and always more time.
  • Access. A tree in a back yard with no gate access costs significantly more than one by the kerb.
  • Lean and condition. A dead or leaning tree is more dangerous to remove.
  • Volume of debris. Hauling away a large canopy is a real cost, and some quotes exclude it.
  • Whether the tree is protected. See below.
Some Central Texas cities protect large trees. Austin and several neighbouring jurisdictions have ordinances covering certain protected species and heritage trees, and removing one without a permit can carry a substantial penalty. Ask your city's code department before work starts, and ask the arborist whether they handle permits.

When a storm takes a tree down

The insurance rule people misunderstand: if a tree falls on your house, your policy generally covers removing the part that hit the structure, and the damage. It typically does not cover removing the rest of the tree from your yard, and it usually does not cover a tree that fell in the yard without hitting anything. Coverage for debris removal typically has a cap.

  • Document before you clear anything, with dated photographs.
  • Protect the property first, then photograph again.
  • Notify your insurer promptly. Most policies expect it.
  • If a neighbour's tree fell on your house, your policy generally responds regardless of who owned the tree. Whether their negligence matters is a separate question, and one for a professional.
  • Check whether your policy covers removal of a dangerous tree before it falls. Some do, up to a limit.

Choosing a tree service

  • Ask whether they carry general liability insurance and workers' compensation, and ask to see certificates.
  • Ask whether a certified arborist assesses the job, or a salesperson.
  • Get the debris removal and stump grinding stated explicitly in the quote.
  • Ask who is responsible if a dropped limb damages the roof or a fence.
  • Be wary of door-knockers after a storm, unmarked vehicles, and large cash deposits.

Better than removing it

If the concern is limbs over a roof, the answer is often pruning rather than felling, and it is a fraction of the cost. Have an arborist assess whether the tree can be made safe before you commission removal. On a live oak especially, a well-pruned tree is usually more valuable to your property than an empty yard.
