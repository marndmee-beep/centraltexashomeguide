Water softener cost: is it worth it here?

The water in much of Central Texas is hard enough to shorten the life of your water heater and appliances. Here's what softening costs and what it returns.

The short answer

A whole-home water softener installed runs $1,500 to $4,000, with most homes landing around $2,000 to $2,800. Running costs are roughly $100 to $250 a year in salt and a little extra water. Under-sink reverse osmosis for drinking water is $300 to $900 installed, with filters around $50 to $120 a year.

How hard is the water here?

Much of Central Texas draws from limestone aquifers and the water is genuinely hard — commonly in the range where you'd expect scale to build up on fixtures, in kettles and inside the water heater. That matters more here than in most places because the water heater works year-round, and the scale that settles inside it acts as an insulating layer between the flame and the water, so you burn more gas to get the same hot water.

You can check your own water with a cheap test kit, or look up your supplier's published water quality report, which most utilities post online. Start there rather than taking a salesman's word for it.

Soften the hot side, filter the cold. A common and sensible setup is a softener for the whole house to protect the water heater and appliances, plus a small reverse osmosis unit at the kitchen tap for drinking water, since softened water adds a little sodium.

What it actually protects

  • Your water heater, which is the single clearest saving. Scale shortens its life by years, and it's a $1,200 to $2,600 replacement.
  • Dishwasher and washing machine, both of which scale up and fail earlier without soft water.
  • Fixtures and showerheads, which clog and lose flow.
  • Soap and detergent use, which drops noticeably once water is soft.

The honest payback

Softening rarely pays for itself on energy savings alone inside five years. The case is stronger when you add appliance life: a water heater lasting 14 years instead of nine, plus a dishwasher and a washing machine that reach their expected life, adds up to more than the install cost over a decade. If your water is very hard and your plumbing is old, the case is clear. If your water is only moderately hard, it's a comfort purchase, and there's nothing wrong with saying so.

Worth knowing before you buy

  • A softener needs a drain and power near where it's installed. Retrofit into a full garage or closet can add cost.
  • Some municipalities restrict brine discharge from regeneration into storm drains. Check locally.
  • Softened water is not filtered water. They solve different problems, and many homes need both.
  • Renting is an option at around $30 to $60 a month, which sounds cheap until you compare it to buying outright over five years.

Questions to ask

  • What grain capacity do you recommend for my household size, and why?
  • Does the price include the drain, the bypass valve and the install labour?
  • What's the warranty on the control valve and the tank?
  • Will you test my water first, or are you assuming what it is?
