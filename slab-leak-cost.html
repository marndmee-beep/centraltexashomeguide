Slab leak detection and repair cost

Water under a concrete slab is one of the most expensive surprises a homeowner can face here. Here's how it's found, what it costs, and when to slow down.

Advertisement

The short answer

Detecting a slab leak in the Austin and San Antonio area costs $300 to $800. Repair runs $800 to $3,500 for a spot repair, and $4,000 to $15,000 if the plumber reroutes the line through the attic or walls instead of breaking out the floor. Thousands of Central Texas homes were built on slab foundations between 1970 and 2005, and the copper supply lines in those homes are now at the age where pinhole leaks appear.

The signs

  • A water bill that jumps with no change in how many people are in the house. This is the most reliable signal, and the easiest to check.
  • The sound of running water when every tap and appliance is off.
  • A warm spot on the floor. Under a hot water line, that's close to conclusive.
  • Cracks that appear and spread in tile or slab, especially in a line.
  • Damp carpet, lifting flooring, or a musty smell with no visible source.
  • Your water meter moving while nothing is running.
Do the meter test before you call anyone. Shut off every tap, appliance and irrigation zone, then look at the meter's leak indicator. If it's moving, water is escaping somewhere. That one check tells you whether you have a real problem and stops a hard sell on work you may not need.

Why it happens here

Two things combine. The soil in this region shifts with moisture, and a slab sitting on moving clay flexes. Copper supply lines buried in that slab do not flex with it, and after 25 or 30 years they crack. Add hard, slightly acidic water moving through them, and pinhole leaks follow. Homes built in the 1980s and 1990s across north Austin, Round Rock and San Antonio's newer suburbs are the peak of that risk.

Detecting without tearing up the floor

A competent plumber locates the leak before breaking concrete. Methods include acoustic listening, pressure isolation of individual lines, thermal imaging, and in some cases a small camera. Ask which method will be used. "We'll open up the floor and find it" is not detection, and it's expensive.

Repair, reroute, or re-pipe?

  • One leak in an otherwise sound system: spot repair is reasonable, though it means breaking out and patching concrete.
  • A second leak, or a system over 30 years old: rerouting through the attic avoids breaking the floor at all, and is often the better long-term answer.
  • Multiple leaks on different lines: a whole-home re-pipe is the honest recommendation. Patching one leak at a time on an ageing system is throwing money at a losing battle.
Where people get oversold: a company that jumps straight to a full re-pipe when you have a single, newly detected leak on a young system. Get a second opinion, and ask both plumbers how many leaks the system has had.

Questions to ask

  • Will you confirm the location before breaking any concrete?
  • Is your detection fee credited against the repair if I hire you?
  • Is the repair warrantied, and for how long?
  • If the wall or floor is opened, who patches and repaints it?
  • Should I file a homeowners insurance claim? Ask your own insurer — many policies cover the resulting damage but not the pipe repair itself.
