Quoting basics for Central Texas contractors

The job usually goes to the contractor who quoted first and quoted clearly. Here's how to do both without extra effort.

Advertisement

Speed beats price more often than people think

Homeowners call two or three companies after something breaks. The first written quote that lands, while the problem is still fresh, sets the anchor for every quote that arrives afterwards. A quote sent from the driveway while you're still on site beats a tidier one that arrives on Thursday.

Split materials from labour

One lump sum gives the customer nothing to judge, and it makes every line of the job negotiable. Materials and labour on separate lines shows them where the money goes, and it protects you on a job where the customer changes something partway through.

Write down your assumptions

  • Access available during working hours
  • Existing wiring, joists or pipework is sound
  • Customer supplies tiles, paint or fixtures
  • Price excludes permit fees and any remediation found after opening up

This matters here. Foundations move and HVAC systems hide surprises in the attic. If access or remediation is rare in your jobs, say so honestly in the quote and handle the exception when it comes.

State what's excluded

Waste removal, making good, and painting are the three most common things customers assume are included. Put them in writing either way.

Add an expiry

Material prices do move, especially on roofing and HVAC equipment. "Price held for 14 days" gives the customer a reason to decide without pressure, and protects you from a quote given in spring being treated as valid in autumn.

Ask for a deposit on anything sizeable

Thirty percent to book the work, balance on completion, is a normal and defensible structure here. It filters out customers who were never going to commit, and it covers the materials you have to buy up front.

Make it look like a company, not a text message

The quote is often the only document a customer sees from you before deciding. A clean PDF with your business name, contact details, an itemised breakdown and a clear total signals that you run a business rather than picking up work between jobs. That perception is worth real money on jobs over a few thousand dollars.

Get a number on every document. Sequential numbering — quote 041, invoice 042 — is what makes chasing a late payment straightforward six weeks later, and it's what any accountant will ask you for at tax time.

Keeping the paperwork straight

  • Save every quote and invoice as a PDF, named with the number and the customer.
  • Keep a simple log: number, customer, job, amount, date sent, date paid.
  • Send a reminder the day before payment is due. It's ordinary, not aggressive.
  • Once a job is over a couple of thousand dollars, confirm variations in writing even when it's a text message you screenshot and save.
