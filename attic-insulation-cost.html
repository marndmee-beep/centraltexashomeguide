Attic insulation cost and cooling savings

In a place where the air conditioning runs nine months a year, the attic is where your money escapes. What fixing it costs, and what it actually returns.

The short answer

Blowing additional insulation into an existing Central Texas attic costs $1,500 to $4,500 for a typical home, including air sealing around recessed lights, ducts and top plates. Air sealing alone, without adding insulation, can run $600 to $1,800 and often delivers more benefit per dollar than the insulation itself.

Why the attic matters more here

Attic air in Central Texas can reach 140°F in August. Every gap between the attic and the living space is a hole that hot air pours through, and your air conditioner pays to remove that heat. The ductwork in most of these homes also runs through the attic, so any duct leak is leaking cooled air directly into the hottest place in the house. That is why air sealing and duct repair routinely beat insulation in payback here.

Check the ducts first. If your ducts leak, adding insulation above them changes little. A duct leakage test, or a careful visual inspection of every joint and connection with the system running, is worth doing before you spend money on insulation.

What you can expect to save

Honest numbers depend on where you start. A house with almost nothing in the attic can cut its cooling cost by 10 to 20 percent. A house that is already reasonably insulated might see 5 percent. Ask any contractor quoting a saving to show the calculation, not a percentage on a brochure, and ask what they assumed your current R-value is.

  • Biggest return: an attic with less than R-19, or a house with visible gaps around light fixtures and plumbing penetrations.
  • Moderate return: topping up an R-30 attic to R-49.
  • Poor return: radiant barrier on an already well-sealed, well-insulated attic.

Radiant barrier: worth it or not?

Radiant barriers reduce heat radiated down from the roof deck, and in this climate they are more defensible than in cooler states. The catch is that they only perform properly once air sealing is done, and they don't substitute for insulation. If a salesperson leads with a radiant barrier and skips air sealing, that's a sign the visit is about commission rather than your cooling bill.

Rebates and incentives

Some Texas electricity providers offer rebates for verified efficiency work, and a proper job usually involves a before-and-after measurement to qualify. Ask the contractor whether they handle the rebate paperwork, and check your own provider's current programme before you sign.

Questions to ask

  • Will you air seal before insulating? Where, specifically?
  • What R-value will the attic be at when you finish, and what is it now?
  • Are the ducts inspected and sealed, or is that separate?
  • Do you measure before and after, and will I get that in writing?
