Solar panels in Central Texas

Plenty of sun, hot summers and a deregulated electricity market. What solar costs, what it returns, and which sales claims to discount.

The short answer

A typical residential solar installation in the Austin and San Antonio area costs $18,000 to $32,000 before any federal tax credit, for a system sized around 8 to 12 kilowatts. After the federal credit, most homeowners are looking at $12,000 to $23,000 net.

Why the payback is shorter here than in most places

  • Sun. Central Texas gets high solar irradiance year round, so a given system produces more than it would in the north.
  • Air conditioning load. Panels generate most in summer, which is exactly when your cooling bill peaks. That timing matters more than the annual total.
  • Electricity rates. Texas rates have risen sharply in recent years, which improves the economics of generating your own.
  • The federal credit is the main incentive, and eligibility depends on your tax situation.

What actually drives the numbers

Payback depends on four things, and any quote that skips them is a sales pitch rather than an analysis:

  • Your real electricity usage, taken from twelve months of bills, not an estimate.
  • Your roof's orientation and shading. A south-facing roof with no tree cover performs far better than a west-facing roof under a live oak.
  • How your utility credits exported power. In Texas this varies enormously between providers, and it changes the maths completely.
  • Your roof's remaining life. Putting panels on a roof that needs replacing in five years means paying to remove and refit them.
The roof first, then the panels. If your roof is within five years of replacement, deal with the roof first. Removal and reinstallation costs $1,500 to $4,000 and is money spent for nothing.

Claims to treat with suspicion

  • "Your bill will go to zero." Rarely true on a grid-tied system without a battery and a very favourable export rate.
  • A payback under five years. Ask for the underlying numbers, including your usage and the export rate assumed.
  • "Sign today for this price." Solar pricing is not a one-day offer.
  • A lease or power purchase agreement explained as free. It is a long contract with an escalator, and it can complicate selling the house.
  • Financing quoted as a monthly payment only. Ask for the total cost of the system and the interest rate separately.

Before you sign

  • Get three quotes, each listing panel and inverter model numbers, wattage and warranty terms.
  • Ask for a production estimate in kilowatt hours per year, and the assumptions behind it.
  • Confirm who pulls the permit and who handles the utility interconnection paperwork.
  • Ask what happens if the installer goes out of business. Who honours the workmanship warranty?
  • Check whether your homeowners insurer needs to be told. Most do.
  • Ask your accountant about the federal credit before you rely on it.
