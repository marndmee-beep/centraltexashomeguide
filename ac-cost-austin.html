Air conditioning replacement cost in Austin

A straight answer on what a new system costs here, and why the number you're quoted varies so much between companies.

Advertisement

The short answer

For a typical single-family home in the Austin area, replacing a central air conditioning system runs $6,000 to $12,000 installed. A 3-ton system, which suits roughly 1,500 to 2,000 square feet, usually lands between $7,500 and $9,500 for mid-range equipment. High-efficiency systems and anything needing ductwork work push past that.

What makes up the price

Part of the jobShare of the bill
Equipment, condenser and air handler45–55%
Labour and installation25–35%
Line set, pad, fittings and materials5–10%
Permits and inspection2–5%
Ductwork repair or replacement, if needed0–30%

Equipment is the largest line, and it's where quotes differ most. Two companies quoting the "same" 3-ton system may be offering different efficiency ratings, different warranties and different brands.

What pushes the price up here

  • Attic installations. Most Central Texas homes put the air handler in the attic. Summer attic temperatures make the work slower and harder, and that labour shows up in your quote.
  • Ductwork in bad shape. If your ducts leak or are undersized, a new condenser won't fix your comfort problem. Expect $1,500 to $4,000 to address ducts.
  • Old R-22 systems. If you're replacing a system installed before 2010, the new equipment uses a different refrigerant and the line set may need replacing too.
  • Two-story homes. Two systems, or a zoning setup, roughly doubles equipment cost.
  • Emergency replacement in July. Companies charge more when everyone's AC has failed at once. If you can plan the replacement in spring or autumn, you'll pay less.
The efficiency trade-off. Higher SEER2 ratings cost more up front and save less than the sales pitch suggests in a mild month — but in Central Texas you run the system most of the year, so the payback is faster here than almost anywhere. Ask what the actual electricity saving is at your usage, in dollars, not percentages.

Repair or replace?

The common rule is to replace when a repair costs more than a third of a new system, or when the unit is past 12 years old. In this climate, age matters more than the rule suggests. See our full guide on repairing versus replacing.

How to get a fair quote

  • Get three quotes in writing, each listing the model number, SEER2 rating and warranty terms.
  • Ask for a manual J load calculation. A company sizing your system by square footage alone is guessing.
  • Check the contractor's licence and insurance. Texas requires an air conditioning contractor licence.
  • Ask what's excluded: ductwork, permits, hauling away the old unit, thermostat.
