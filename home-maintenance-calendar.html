The Central Texas home maintenance calendar

Houses here face a specific set of pressures: extreme heat, spring storms, expanding clay and dry summers. This is the order to tackle them in.

February and March: before the heat

  • Service the air conditioning. A spring service catches a failing capacitor or a blocked condensate drain before July, when everyone else is calling too. It also keeps a new system inside its warranty.
  • Change the filter and note the date. In a dusty Texas spring, filters load faster than the box suggests.
  • Check the attic while it's still cool enough to be up there. Look for daylight, stained decking, and disconnected ducts.
  • Clean the gutters and check that downspouts discharge well away from the foundation.

April and May: storm season

  • Photograph your roof before storm season. A dated set of photos is the best evidence you can have if a claim follows.
  • Trim trees back from the roof and remove dead limbs over the house.
  • Check the grading. After spring rain, walk the perimeter and note where water stands. Standing water next to a slab is the beginning of a foundation problem.
  • After any storm, look at gutters, vents, skylights and downspouts for dents, and check the ceiling inside for staining.

June, July and August: survival mode

  • Water the foundation. A soaker hose on a timer around the perimeter, run in the early morning, keeps the clay from shrinking away from the slab. This is the cheapest foundation maintenance that exists.
  • Flush the water heater once a year to remove scale. It's the single best thing you can do for a tank in hard water.
  • Watch the water bill. A jump with no change in habits is the first sign of a leak.
  • Don't set the thermostat back aggressively for long periods in peak heat. The system works harder to recover than it saved, and it runs longer at the worst possible time.
If the AC fails in August, expect higher prices and longer waits. Companies charge more when demand peaks. If you know your system is at the end of its life, replacing it in March or April is meaningfully cheaper.

September and October: recovery and preparation

  • Get the roof inspected after storm season, before any claim window closes. Most policies expect prompt reporting.
  • Re-check the grading and add soil where it has settled away from the slab.
  • Service the heating side of the system if you have a heat pump or furnace.
  • Check weatherstripping on doors and windows before the few cold weeks arrive.

November, December and January: the quiet months

  • Know where your water shut-off is and make sure everyone in the house does too. In a hard freeze, a burst pipe does thousands of dollars of damage in minutes.
  • Protect exterior pipes before the first freeze warning. Foam covers cost a few dollars each.
  • Drain the irrigation system if a freeze is forecast.
  • Re-seal exterior wood and repaint where it's flaking. UV is hard on exposed timber here.

The two things that matter most

  • Water management around the foundation. Consistent soil moisture prevents most of the region's expensive structural problems.
  • Annual air conditioning service. It's the difference between a system that lasts 15 years and one that fails at 11.
